# Q-based super query: retrieval diagnostic (2026-09-25, PI[claude])

Script: scripts/03_query_diag.py. Log: outputs/01_needle/03_query_diag.log.
4 needle words × layers 19/23/27/31 × 16 heads. Metric: rank of the needle key in each construction's attention weights (no patching).

Observed:
- The model's own recall query ("…Quick reminder, the secret word is") puts the needle top-1 in 66% of heads. The direction exists.
- Only 13% (median) of (recall q − mean q) lies in the top-4 PCs of Q.
- Best-of-R top-1 is lenient: random query banks of the same size match SVD/PCA banks (±8: random 0.40, SVD QKᵀ 0.37; ±16: 0.69 vs 0.88).
- After max-pooling the bank into one weight vector, every Q construction selects the needle in 0–3% of heads (random 4–5%).
- meanQ native 12%, softOR (logsumexp of raw logits over queries) 9%, normal last query 12%.
- farA (max over queries of A, the method that works downstream) also has the needle top-1 in only 12% of heads.

Inference (unverified): farA's downstream effect may be indirect. Its picks include "secret", "word", "Remember", which may switch the model into "talk about the secret word" mode; the model's own attention then fetches the needle. Test queued: pueue 2136 (rerun) vs 2137 (--exclude_needle).

Oracles (slop/reviews/20260925_super-query-oracle_*.md) agree: averaging queries gives the consensus, not the union; re-rotating queries to the last position is wrong; averaging R retrievals dilutes; pool after each query's softmax.
